Discussion questions · Think, then reveal

01
Learning objective 12.1.A

What is a magnetic field?

A magnetic field is a vector field that can be used to determine the magnetic force exerted on moving electric charges, electric currents, or magnetic materials. Its direction and magnitude depend on position. A moving charge parallel to the field experiences no magnetic force.

02
Learning objective 12.1.A

What kinds of objects can experience magnetic forces?

(a) Moving charged particles (such as electrons or protons); (b) current-carrying conductors (wires); and (c) magnetic materials, whose electron-associated magnetic moments can interact with a field.

03
Learning objective 12.1.A

What is a magnetic dipole?

A magnetic dipole is a magnetic system with both a north and a south pole. A bar magnet is a dipole, and electrons have magnetic dipole moments associated with their spin (and orbital states). An isolated magnetic north or south pole—a magnetic monopole—has not been observed.

04
Learning objective 12.1.B

What happens if a bar magnet is broken into two pieces?

Each piece has a north and a south pole: two smaller magnetic dipoles are formed. Breaking a magnet cannot separate a north magnetic pole from a south magnetic pole.

05
Learning objective 12.1.B

How do magnetic poles of the same and opposite polarity interact?

Like poles repel (N–N or S–S). Unlike poles attract (N–S).

06
Learning objective 12.1.A

What do magnetic field lines show?

(a) The direction of the field at a point: a small compass needle tends to point tangentially to a line, with its north-seeking end pointing along the field. (b) Line spacing indicates relative field strength in a given field map: closer lines indicate a stronger field. (c) For the direction of force on a moving charge, we must also know the charge’s velocity and sign; the force is not simply along the field line.

07
Learning objective 12.1.A

What is meant by saying magnetic field lines form closed loops?

They have no beginning or end. For a bar magnet, the lines go north to south outside the magnet and south to north inside it, completing continuous loops.

08
Learning objective 12.1.A

What factors affect the strength of a magnetic field?

Magnetic field strength depends on the source (for example, the strength of a magnet or the current producing the field), distance and position relative to the source, and the magnetic properties of any surrounding material. The dependence is different for different field configurations.

09
Learning objective 12.1.B

How does the field strength of a magnetic dipole change with distance?

The magnitude of the magnetic field decreases as distance from the dipole increases. This is a qualitative requirement in Topic 12.1.

10
Learning objective 12.1.B

What happens when a compass or other magnetic dipole is placed in an external magnetic field?

The magnetic field exerts a turning effect (torque) on a misaligned dipole, so it tends to rotate until it aligns with the field. The compass north-seeking end points along the field.

11
Learning objective 12.1.B

How do permanent magnetism and induced magnetism differ?

Both arise from the collective alignment of magnetic dipoles. With permanent magnetism, significant alignment remains after the external field is removed. With induced magnetism, an external magnetic field produces or changes magnetization; the alignment often weakens when the field is removed (though some may remain).

12
Learning objective 12.1.C

What is meant by magnetic permeability?

Magnetic permeability describes how a material responds magnetically to an external magnetic field: it is a measure of the magnetization produced in a material in response to an applied field.

13
Learning objective 12.1.C

What is the symbol for magnetic permeability?

The Greek letter μ (mu). Free-space (vacuum) permeability is represented by μ0.

14
Learning objective 12.1.C

What is meant by the permeability of free space?

The vacuum permeability, μ0, is the constant value of magnetic permeability associated with empty space (a vacuum). It appears in magnetic-field equations.

15
Learning objective 12.1.C

Look up the value of the permeability of free space in the AP Physics 2 equation booklet.

μ0 = 4π × 10−7 (T·m)/A (exactly as printed in the College Board table of information).

16
Learning objective 12.1.C

What factors affect the permeability of a material?

A material’s magnetic permeability depends on its composition and internal arrangement and can vary with temperature, orientation, and the strength of the external magnetic field. It is not generally a fixed constant for a material.

17
Learning objective 12.1.B

What is meant by the term ferromagnetic?

Ferromagnetic materials (for example, iron, nickel, and cobalt) can be strongly magnetized when an external field aligns magnetic domains or atomic magnetic dipoles. They can retain magnetization after the field is removed.

18
Learning objective 12.1.B

What is meant by the term paramagnetic?

Paramagnetic materials (for example, aluminum, titanium, and magnesium) interact weakly with an external field; their magnetic dipoles do not remain aligned after the field is removed.

19
Learning objective 12.1.B

What is meant by the term diamagnetic?

Diamagnetism is a property of all materials: their electronic structure produces a typically weak magnetic response with induced magnetic moments opposing the applied field.

20
Learning objective 12.1.B

How can Earth’s magnetic field be modeled?

Earth’s magnetic field can be approximated by a magnetic dipole. Near geographic north, the field generally points into Earth, so the north-seeking end of a compass points toward a region behaving like a magnetic south pole. Geographic and magnetic poles are not identical.

21
Learning objective 12.1.A

Describe the shape and direction of the magnetic field around a bar magnet.

The magnetic field forms a set of closed loops. Outside the bar magnet, field lines leave the N end, curve around the magnet, and enter the S end. Inside the magnet they return from S to N. The field is stronger where lines are more closely spaced.

22
Bridge to 12.2

Define magnetic field strength for a charged particle moving perpendicular to the field.

For motion at 90° to the magnetic field, its magnitude is the magnetic force per unit magnitude of charge per unit speed: B = FB / (|q|v). This is a useful introduction to Topic 12.2.

23
Bridge to 12.2

What is the SI unit for magnetic field strength?

The tesla (T). Equivalently, 1 T = 1 N/(A·m).
